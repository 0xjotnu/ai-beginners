# Start Here

Welcome. This guide is designed to take you from “I have heard of AI” to confidently using it for real work and small projects.

## How to learn here

Do not try to read everything at once. Pick one AI tool, give it low-risk tasks, and keep a short note of what worked and what did not. Each lesson should take 10–30 minutes, plus practice.

## Choose your path

- **Everyday work or study:** foundations → tools → prompting → automation.
- **Creative work:** foundations → prompting → images, video, and audio.
- **Software and technical projects:** foundations → prompting → AI coding → agents and projects.
- **Just curious:** read the foundations, then choose any topic that sounds useful.

## Your first practice task

Pick a harmless real task: summarize a public article, draft an email, create a study plan, or explain a concept. Use this prompt:

```text
Help me with [task].

Context: [the background it needs]
Audience: [who will use the result]
Success looks like: [what a good result includes]
Format: [bullets, table, email, JSON, etc.]

If information is uncertain, say so and tell me what I should verify.
```

Then improve the prompt once after seeing the result. That small feedback loop is the core skill behind good AI use.

## Safety checklist

Before sharing information with an AI tool, pause if it includes passwords, API keys, private customer data, health details, legal documents, unpublished work, or anything your employer or school restricts. Use approved tools and remove identifying details when possible.
